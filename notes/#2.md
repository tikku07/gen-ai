# Large Language Models (LLMs)

## What is an LLM?

**LLM (Large Language Model)** is an AI model trained on a very large amount of text data to understand and generate human-like language.

LLMs can perform tasks such as:

* Text generation
* Question answering
* Summarization
* Translation
* Code generation
* Text classification
* Conversational AI

Examples include **GPT, Claude, and Gemini**.

---

## GPT Architecture

**GPT** stands for:

> **Generative Pre-trained Transformer**

Each part describes an important characteristic of the model:

* **Generative** – Generates new content.
* **Pre-trained** – Trained on large datasets before being used for specific tasks.
* **Transformer** – Uses the Transformer neural network architecture.

GPT models use the Transformer architecture to process input text and generate output.

---

## Transformer Architecture

The **Transformer** is a neural network architecture introduced in the 2017 research paper *"Attention Is All You Need"* by researchers at Google and the University of Toronto.

Transformers became important in Natural Language Processing because they can efficiently process relationships between different parts of a sequence.

A major component of the Transformer is the **Attention Mechanism**.

---

## Attention Mechanism

Attention allows a model to determine which parts of the input are more relevant to each other when understanding context.

For example:

> "The animal didn't cross the road because **it** was tired."

The model needs to determine what **"it"** refers to. Attention helps the model understand relationships between words and their surrounding context.

### Why Attention is Important

Attention helps models:

* Understand context
* Identify relationships between words
* Handle long-range dependencies
* Determine which tokens are important
* Generate more contextually relevant responses

---

## How LLMs Work

At a simplified level, an LLM generates text by predicting what token is likely to come next based on the tokens that came before it.

For example:

```text
The sky is
```

The model calculates probabilities for possible next tokens:

```text
blue     → high probability
green    → lower probability
computer → very low probability
```

It selects a token according to the model's decoding process and then repeats the process.

```text
The sky is
      ↓
The sky is blue
      ↓
The sky is blue today
      ↓
The sky is blue today because...
```

This process continues until the model reaches an appropriate stopping point or the configured output limit.

> **Important:** LLMs do not simply search for a pre-written answer. They generate output based on patterns learned during training and the context provided to them.

# Tokens and Tokenization

Computers ultimately process information numerically. Before text can be processed by an LLM, it is converted into smaller units called **tokens**.

### What is a Token?

A token can represent:

* A complete word
* Part of a word
* Punctuation
* Spaces or other text components

The exact way text is divided depends on the tokenizer used by the model.
